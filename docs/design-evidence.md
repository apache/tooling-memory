# Appendix G: Evidence

Draft, 2 November 2026. A companion to [*Durable Memory for Disposable Agents*](durable-memory-for-disposable-agents.md). It may be moved into the paper as Appendix G or stay beside it.

## Purpose, and how to read it

The paper states each property of the design once, with at most a citation or two, and keeps its reasoning short. This appendix lists every property the paper commits to (a principle, a rule, a mechanism, a number) and says, for each, why it is there, what evidence stands behind it, how strong that evidence is, and what observation would make the paper revise or drop it. It exists so that when new research arrives, a reader can find the row it bears on and judge the paper's position without re-deriving it.

Each row links to the paper's heading and gives the line number in brackets, against the text at commit `2b6cc5a`. Citations use the paper's reference numbers and link to its reference list. A quotation or number appears only where the source's full text was opened in this session and the number was found there with its conditions (benchmark, models, sample size, mean or maximum). Where only an abstract could be read, the cell says "abstract only" and the row is not rated above Single study on that source. Where the source could not be opened, the cell says so, and confidence is judged from the source's standing. Where a property rests on the paper's own reasoning, the cell says that plainly. Several sources may support one property; where two confidence levels apply, the stronger is given and the other is mentioned.

## The confidence scale

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Established** | Long-standing practice or standards with wide adoption, such as leases, event sourcing, or partial evaluation in a policy engine |
| **Replicated** | Measured by two or more independent groups |
| **Single study** | One paper or report, however good. Single-author work and a group's own benchmark are noted |
| **Vendor report** | A vendor's own account of its own system |
| **Argued** | Rests on reasoning in the paper, not on external evidence |
| **Incident** | Grounded in a documented real-world incident |

The levels are about the evidence, not about whether the property is right. A rule that is Argued may be sound; it is simply unmeasured. The last section lists the Argued rows that matter most, so the paper's exposure is visible in one place.

## Where the design rests on argument alone

Most of the design's structure is Argued: it follows from a few grounded premises (the incidents, trust non-transitivity, authority collapse, revocation failure, fast-falling prices) by reasoning the paper lays out but does not measure. That is expected of a design paper. The rows below are the Argued ones where a contrary observation would cost the most, in rough order of exposure.

1. **Agents are stateless and disposable; the handoff note carries all continuity** [70, 110, 621]. Everything else assumes this. It now has support from practice [109], from a study of consolidated restatements on chat tasks [110], and from hospital handoffs [111], but no study compares an agent's handoff note with transcript replay. If long-lived agents with private state did measurably better work than a chain of cold starts reading handoffs, the ledger, leases, and the write path would all need rethinking. The §11 arms can test it, but no result exists yet.
2. **Four labels, never one score** [272–283]. The design's trust model is its most distinctive part and the costliest to enforce. The evidence that composite scores fail ([8], and the laundering channels in [81]) is one study each, on synthetic benchmarks. A composite shown safe across poisoning, leakage, and revocation suites would simplify the whole of §4.
3. **Reading costs, and the discipline holds even if reading becomes free** [41]. No longer Argued: three groups find accuracy falling as input grows with the evidence present [101]–[103]. It stays on this list because the size of the effect varies widely by model and task, and some current models show none on simple lookups. If that becomes general, the T0 cap, summaries-first retrieval, and the recall deputy survive on cost alone.
4. **Independent auditors on a different model where a capable one is available** [382–393]. No longer Argued for the model: self-preference [104] and failed self-correction [105] support a different model, and correlated errors [106]–[108] bound what it buys. Two studies [112], [113] add that a weaker reviewer can make work worse and that a fresh same-model review finds errors a cross-model review misses, so the rule now asks for capability and falls back to a fresh context; both studies are small. If same-model audit caught as many seeded faults, the design would keep auditors but lose the independence rule.
5. **Curation with a protected floor** [361], and **a summary rebuilt from its sources each time** [365]. The premise (repeated incremental rewriting loses information) is Replicated. The remedy is not: no study has shown that rebuild-from-sources curation keeps memory above the no-memory floor over years, and [85] found that static consolidation of a whole pool held at 100% while streaming updates degraded, which is closer to the paper's batched materializer than to its curator loop. The raw-only arm [821] is the paper's own test, and it has not been run.
6. **One memory server per session** [432–440]. Grounded in one incident [89]. If protocols gained a way to enforce one filter across servers, the rule would soften to a preference.
7. **The horizon scanner is a person** [501–509]. The design puts outward-looking judgment with a named human and gives no mechanism for it beyond a budget and a review. This is where the paper most openly declines to automate, and it has no evidence either way.
8. **Spend and throughput as two separate limits** [922–934]. The reasoning is clear and the practice (quotas) is common, but no fleet has been shown to need both; if capacity becomes elastic the throughput limit may never bind.

The counts by confidence level, from the 132 rows above, taking the stronger level where a row names two (in the order Established, Replicated, Single study, Incident, Vendor report, Argued): Argued 62, Established 27, Single study 22, Incident 10, Replicated 7, Vendor report 4. The Argued rows are just under half the design. The grounded rows are concentrated in §4, §5, and §12, which is where the paper puts its citations, and absent from §7, §8's rules, and most of the appendices, which is where a reader should expect to be asked to trust the reasoning.

## References that do not carry their claim

Each item names a reference, where the paper uses it, and the gap. Most are small. None invalidates the row it sits in; each should be re-worded or re-sourced.

- **[11] RFC 4151, line 858.** The paper quotes "ownership of domains and email addresses is subject to change". The RFC's full text was searched and contains neither "subject to change" nor "ownership of domains". The closest sentence is "Domain names change hands and the new assignee of a domain name can't be sure that they are minting new names." The point stands; the quotation does not. Corrected in the paper at 0df5c4b: it now quotes "domain names change hands".
- **[84] Poikarayil et al., line 361.** The paper says "a stale claim misleads more than a missing one". The study has no missing-fact condition: its swap replaces an unrelated statement with the obsolete one while the current statement stays retrieved. It shows that a stale claim misleads even beside the current one, which is the stronger and better-supported sentence. Corrected in the paper at e58aad5: it now says "a stale claim misleads even when the current one is retrieved beside it".
- **[85] Zhang et al., line 365.** The paper cites it for "rewriting a rewrite loses detail with each pass". The body shows degradation under per-problem streaming updates on a 19-problem slice, with the headline loss given as 54%, 46%, and 52.6% in different places, while static consolidation of the whole pool held at 100%. The citation supports "repeated incremental rewriting loses detail"; it does not support a claim about rewriting in general. Corrected in the paper at 0df5c4b: it now says "repeated incremental rewriting loses detail".
- **[91] Shen et al., line 720.** The paper's sentence, "stores that leave this to the prompt keep serving revoked records", is carried. The abstract's "no system enforces revocation by default" is not: in the body, mem0's default read-time filter removed the expired record under direct insertion. Anyone lifting the abstract's phrase into the paper should not.
- **[34] AgentPoison, line 945.** Cited for poisoning "through stores", which it supports. Its headline "over 80%" is retrieval success; end-to-end success averages about 62.6%, and one of its three poison rates (about 0.28%) exceeds the "under 0.1%" it claims. Any number taken from its abstract should be the end-to-end one.
- **[16] Strathern, line 919.** The paper cites the phrasing "when a measure becomes a target it stops being a good measure". The article's abstract was opened and does not contain it; the body was not opened. The phrasing is widely attributed to this paper and probably in it, but it was not verified here. Corrected in the paper at 0df5c4b: it now uses the wording "ceases to be a good measure".
- **[67] SEAGym, line 732.** The paper says recent work on self-evolving agents "finds the same, that most gains come from improving the harness, without retraining". SEAGym's full text states that premise in its abstract, confines its experiments to "model-external harness evolution", and lists weight updates as future work. It measures harness evolution's reliability; it does not compare harness gains with retraining. "Finds" should be "starts from", or the citation should move to a paper that measures the comparison. Corrected in the paper at 0df5c4b: it now says the work "starts from the same premise".
- **[57] LoCoMo, line 949.** Cited for "token cost as an axis" together with [56]. The LoCoMo abstract describes a benchmark of conversations of about 300 turns over up to 35 sessions and does not mention cost (abstract only). [56] carries the cost claim alone. Corrected in the paper at 0df5c4b: it now cites it among the long-horizon benchmarks.
- **[42] Constitutional AI, line 947.** Cited for "written, layered values". The abstract supports written values ("a list of rules or principles"); nothing in it is layered (abstract only). The layering is the paper's own. Corrected in the paper at 0df5c4b: it now cites it for "written values".
- **[94] Huang et al., line 943.** Cited with [93] for "surveys finding the governance of persistent state the least studied part". In the full text read (abstract, early body, and sections 9.1 to 10), the survey raises "memory governance and coordination challenges" in multi-entity settings (§9.2) and calls for "trustworthy memory infrastructures" (§9.4), as needs rather than as the least studied part; §4.2.3 was not reached. [93] carries the claim (435 works coded: 269 on retrieval, 200 on writing, 27 on rollback; governance deliberately oversampled).
- **[98] OWASP Agent Memory Guard, line 1169.** The reference calls it an "incubator project". The project page opened in this session classifies it as a Lab Project and a Tool. Corrected in the reference.
- **[62] OpenTelemetry, lines 798 and 1097.** The cited page is a moved notice; the conventions now live in a separate repository that states no stability level. The paper's "today" is right; the URL should follow the move. Corrected in the paper at 0df5c4b: it now points to the new repository.
- **[39] OPA, line 419.** The page shows partial evaluation producing conditions translated into SQL. The paper's stronger statement, that unauthorised entries are excluded "before scoring and counting", is implied by the SQL translation and is the paper's inference, not the page's text.
- **[17] Intercom, line 919.** Cited for "vendors of agents are moving the same way, pricing per resolved outcome". The post is one vendor reporting that resolutions were the most preferred metric in its own buyer research. It supports one vendor, not a movement. Corrected in the paper at 0df5c4b: it now says "one vendor of agents reports that its buyers prefer pricing per resolved outcome".
- **References [37] and [64], lines 1047 and 1101.** Both entries lack authors. [37] is by Margalit, Cohen-Inger, Avram, Taig, and Margalit; [64] is by Kaliyev and Maryanskyy, according to their arXiv pages opened in this session. Corrected in the paper at 0df5c4b: it now gives both authors.
